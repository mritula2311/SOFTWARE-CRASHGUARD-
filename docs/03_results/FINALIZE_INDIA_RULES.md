## 2. The evaluated duty model against the limit

Every simulation enforces an hourly allowance of 1% (36 s per hour), stricter than the 10% of row 1 (360 s per hour if the 10% is read over one hour). Two readings of the undefined observation interval:

1. **Per hour (or any long interval).** The evaluated allowance is inside the limit; no result changes.
2. **Per transmission** (the strictest reading, an off-time of T_air (1/d - 1) after each frame on the shared channel). With d = 10% the condition A_f <= d (T_F - s_f) of `DELTA_DUTY_LOCKOUT.md` section 3 allows a flash airtime of at most 175.0 ms (median decision time) or 168.8 ms (mean). It holds for the 14-byte flash up to SF9 (median) and SF9 (mean). With the 8-byte micro frame the micro scheme reaches the base before the single-stage alert (condition C) at SF7 to SF9: SF7 yes, SF8 yes, SF9 yes. The load gate of the gating rule (R6-7) still gives SF7 only.

So the two-stage alert stays feasible at 433 MHz in India under either reading of the 10%. Under a strict 1% per transmission (some other regions) the condition fails at every spreading factor (largest admissible flash airtime 17.5 ms; `max_sf_condition_a.d1` = 0), and the stored two-stage results do not transfer. The number of channels the node may use is not limited by row 1 (no channel plan is given); the simulations use one channel.

## 3. Transmit power and e.r.p.

An assumption of 10 dBm transmit power does not match the code: the simulators use a transmit power of 14 dBm (`ptx_dbm`; ASSUMPTIONS.md) and subtract an extra loss of 10 dB in the link budget (`extra_loss_db`; `xsim_alert.cc` subtracts 10 dB as well). Which of the two figures is the radiated power depends on what the 10 dB stands for; both cases follow. e.r.p. (dBm) = transmit power + antenna gain (dBi) - cable and connector loss (dB) - 2.15; the limit is 10 mW = 10 dBm.

| Case | e.r.p. (dBm) | e.r.p. (mW) | Reduction of transmit power needed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 14 dBm at the antenna, 0 dBi, no loss | 11.85 | 15.3 | 1.85 dB |
| 14 dBm at the antenna, 2.15 dBi (dipole), no loss | 14.00 | 25.1 | 4.00 dB |
| 14 dBm at the radio, 3 dBi antenna, 1 dB cable loss | 13.85 | 24.3 | 3.85 dB |
| the 10 dB extra loss of the link budget is all transmit-side, 0 dBi | 1.85 | 1.53 | none |

Reading. If the 10 dB extra loss is transmit-side (antenna efficiency, cable, mounting), the radiated power is 1.9 dBm (1.5 mW), inside the limit, and the link budget of the simulations stands. If instead the radio delivers 14 dBm to the antenna and the 10 dB is path loss, the 10 mW limit needs the transmit power reduced by 1.85 dB for a 0 dBi antenna and by 4.00 dB for a dipole; every dB of reduction lowers the received level by 1 dB, so the link margin shrinks by the same amount, which is about 0.7 spreading-factor step for the 0 dBi case at the simulators' sensitivity step of about 2.8 dB per step. This is an analytical note: no simulation was rerun with a reduced power, and the author must decide which reading of the 10 dB holds for the hardware.
