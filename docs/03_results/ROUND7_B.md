## 2. Design

Pseudonym p = the leading L bytes of HMAC-SHA256(k_v, "P" || boot (u32) || ctr (u32)); L = 2 where the frame has the 16-bit short id, L = 4 where it has the 32-bit node id. The base keeps for each vehicle the pseudonyms of the next m = 16 counters of its boot and a hash index from pseudonym to the entries; a frame whose pseudonym matches is verified with the tag under each candidate entry (the tag decides); the window moves to the matched counter. **The tag covers the frame bytes and, as associated data that is not sent, boot and counter of the entry.** Without that, two entries of one vehicle with equal pseudonyms are ambiguous: the C benchmark at L = 2 resolved 199,911 of 200,000 frames until the tag covered the counter (fixed; Python and C now resolve all). Desynchronisation: a lost run of more than m frames, or a reboot (the boot counter rises), leaves the frame unresolved; the base then tests the next three boot values for every vehicle (a linear scan, measured below) and a resolved full alert resynchronises the vehicle. Frames that do not resolve or fail the tag trigger only cell-level rate limiting. The boot-counter rule, the seen-set and the per-vehicle quarantine act on the resolved real id, so linkability exists for the base only.

Position: unlinkable ids do not hide position. The position fields of the alert are encrypted (stream cipher keyed from k_v with the nonce boot and counter, authenticated by the existing 8-byte tag: no byte is added). The flash carries at most a 2-byte coarse cell. Responders get the position from the base, not from the air. A 16-byte AEAD tag instead of the 8-byte tag would add 8 bytes: 10.2 ms at SF7, 20.5 ms at SF8, 41.0 ms at SF9 (ANALYTICAL). Out of scope: radio fingerprinting, timing correlation, an attacker that holds a vehicle key.

P = 1 - (1 - 2^-8L)^((n - 1) m) with n = 1,000, m = 16. Enumeration with real HMAC pseudonyms (200,000 frames, seed 42): 0.2195 at L = 2 and 0.0000 at L = 4 (no collision in the sample; the expected count is 0.74). H-R7-B2 (P at most 1e-6 at the frame with the smallest L, L = 2): **not supported**, and not supported at the alert's L = 4 either; the bound is met from L = 5. A collision is resolved by the tag (one more tag check per candidate; wrong attribution needs a tag collision, 2^-64 per candidate for the 8-byte tag).

## 6. Limits

- The proofs are symbolic and need perfect cryptography; they say nothing about timing correlation, radio fingerprints, packet lengths or an attacker with a vehicle key.
- The window is abstracted as an entry for every alert the vehicle sends: a vehicle that falls out of the window (more than m lost frames) or reboots is outside the proofs (the scan above handles it in the implementation).
- The collision bound fails at the frame sizes of the layout; keeping the sizes means a tag check per collision candidate.
- The injective form of the no-framing lemma is not proved.
