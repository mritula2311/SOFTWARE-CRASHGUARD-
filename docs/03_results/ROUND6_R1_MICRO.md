## 1. Result in one paragraph

In ns-3 the micro-confirm scheme sends less airtime than R3 in every one of the 16 cells (between 0.46 and 0.77 of R3's airtime) and its delivery is within 0.0025 of R3's in every cell. H-R6-1a (SF9 extra airtime at most 0.40) and H-R6-1b (delivery not lower than R3 by more than 1 point) meet their criteria as defined. Two limits apply. One: H-R6-1a is close to automatic, because the extra airtime against the single-stage alert is negative in 14 of the 16 cells, as the micro-confirm replaces the 97-byte confirm, so the table below also gives the airtime against R3 and per delivered alert. The other: the trace part of H-R6-1b is zero by construction (loss depends on the frame index, not on the frame length), so it adds nothing to the ns-3 evidence. The formal part (H-R6-1c) is in section 4.

## 4. Formal status (H-R6-1c) and limits

The formal part is reported in `docs/03_results/ROUND6_RESULTS.md` (ProVerif and Tamarin models and the control model; outputs in `formal/out/r6/`). The ns-3 frame has 13 bytes (the smallest frame of the LoRaWAN module) and the design frame has 7; at SF7 a 13-byte and a 14-byte frame have the same time on air, so the ns-3 micro frame saves airtime only against the 97-byte confirm. The micro-confirm gives up the delta-V, peak, switch bits and telemetry of the full confirm (a limit, not measured here). The pending-table lifetime (10, 30, 60 s) made no difference in development, because the confirm is always sent within 10 s of the acknowledged flash.
