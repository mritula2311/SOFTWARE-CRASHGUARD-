## 0. Common rules

- **Seeds.** 42 for everything single-seeded; folds use seed 42 plus the repeat index, as in `saturation_splits` (10 repeats of grouped 5-fold). Simulator evaluation: 40 paired scenario seeds per cell (`EVAL_SEED` 3000, hours 0 to 39); tuning, where a study has any, uses seed 1000 and 3 hours on the eight tuning scenarios of `s11_sct` (`TUNE_COMBOS`) and never touches an evaluation cell.
- **Intervals.** 95% percentile bootstrap, 1,000 resamples. Where the unit is a crash test, whole make/model groups are resampled together (the `boot.ci_grouped` rule of the earlier studies), which is more conservative than resampling tests and is the rule used throughout. Where the unit is a simulation seed, seeds are resampled inside each cell (the `s11_sct.agg_diff` rule).
- **p-values.** One-sided bootstrap p-values (share of resamples on the wrong side of the null, floor 0.001 with 1,000 resamples; a printed 0 means below 0.001).
- **Multiplicity.** One Holm correction over the nine hypotheses that have a statistical test: H-A1, H-A2, H-A3, H-B1, H-B2, H-D1, H-D2, H-E1, H-F2 (their p-values are defined in each study). H-B3 (a ratio of gains), H-E2 and H-F1 (formulas) have no p-value and are decided by their stated rule; they are not in the Holm family. A hypothesis is SUPPORTED only if its stated criterion is met AND its Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05. It is NOT SUPPORTED if a stated "counts against" condition holds or the criterion fails clearly. It is MIXED if one part of a two-part criterion holds and the other does not.
  Inside Study A, the baseline comparisons of A4 (4 baselines x 4 splits x 3 ranges) get their own Holm correction and are labelled EXPLORATORY except the one named in H-A1.
- **Reporting.** Every comparison is reported with its interval whether or not it is favourable. A negative result is an acceptable outcome of every study.
- **Prior work.** Jao and Shkel 2022 (triangular-function reconstruction with Gaussian-process regression), Zhang et al. 2026 (peak-aware saturation compensation) and Chan et al. 2023 (spline through clipped segments) are named in the task. Chan et al. 2023 was opened in an earlier round; the other two could not be opened (`docs/06_references/PRIOR_WORK.md`) and are not cited for any result. Their ideas appear only as "our implementation of the idea" baselines (a triangular pulse fit, a Gaussian-process fit over the unclipped samples, and our existing spline). No result of those papers is quoted.

## 1. Study A: tail-aware clipping estimator (MEASURED_SOFTWARE, 176 NHTSA tests)

**Data and splits.** The 176 tests, the sensor model (`severity_study.sensor_signal`, 100 Hz, ranges +-16, +-32, +-64 g) and the cached cells of `saturation_splits`. The primary split is **R**: repeated grouped 5-fold cross-validation on make/model groups, 10 repeats (132 groups), exactly as in N14. Also reported: **G** (vehicle-group hold-out, 58 tests), **A** (make/model hold-out, 63 tests) and **O** (original test-number rule, 58 tests), each with its tuning tests as training data. Everything fitted (including descriptor-based models, quantile models and calibration) uses the training tests of the fold only. The primary range is +-16 g; +-32 g and +-64 g are secondary.

**Reference numbers from the tagged results (split R, +-16 g, absolute delta-V error %):** plain median 25.7, p90 51.0, within 10% 0.068; spline 23.5, 51.8, 0.157; fill 16.5, 52.8, 0.290; fill + ridge ("M+", the earlier primary) 11.9, 58.5, 0.435. (The task text says spline 51.0 at p90; the stored value is 51.0 for plain and 51.8 for spline; the stored values are used.)

**A1 clipping descriptors.** Per event and range, on the pulse samples from the trigger to the pulse end of the severity code, with a sample counted as clipped when |a| reaches 0.7 of the range (the level the earlier study chose; `FINAL_SAT`): clipped duration (ms), number of clipped runs, clipped fraction of the pulse, slope into the earliest clipped run (g/s, last 3 samples before it), slope out of the last clipped run (g/s, the 3 samples after it), pre-clip rise time (ms from the trigger to the earliest clipped sample), area of the unclipped samples (g s), and the area between the clip level and zero over the clipped samples (g s). Report the Spearman correlation of each with the signed log error log(plain / reference) of the plain estimate, on the tuning tests of split G only (training data), with a grouped bootstrap interval. Descriptive.

**A2 deterministic bounds (ANALYTICAL plus empirical).**
- Lower bound: the delta-V of the clipped signal through the same severity code (the plain estimate). Statement tested: if the sensor can only reduce the magnitude of the true acceleration while keeping its sign on each axis, and the processing is the same linear integration, the integral of the clipped signal is not larger than the integral of the unclipped one, so the plain estimate cannot exceed the true delta-V. Conditions: the sign of each axis is unchanged by clipping; the baseline offset and the pulse end are those of the clipped signal; sensor low-pass filtering before and after clipping is linear and the filter is applied identically (here it is applied after clipping, so a small violation by filter ringing is possible). The empirical check counts, per range, tests where plain > reference x 1.00 and where plain > reference x 1.05, and reports where the assumptions fail.
- Upper bound: from the four pulse families of A3 (half-sine, haversine, triangle, trapezoid) fitted to the unclipped pulse samples by the grid search of `saturation.fit_pulse`. For each family, all grid points (start offset, duration) whose cost is at most **2.0 times the minimum cost** of that family (and at least the minimum cost plus a floor of 1e-6) are admitted; the amplitude of each admitted point is the best-fit amplitude, limited by the existing cap of 12 times the range. The upper bound is the largest filled-signal delta-V over all admitted points and families. The factor 2.0 is fixed here and is not tuned.
- Reported per range: share of tests with reference delta-V inside [lower, upper] (all 176 tests; no fitting involved, so no training split is needed), mean width in km/h and in percent of the reference, and the share where the lower bound already exceeds the reference by more than 5%.

**A3 pulse-shape model averaging.** Families: half-sine, haversine, triangle (as in the repo) and a symmetric trapezoid (rise over the opening quarter, plateau, fall over the last quarter). For each family the filled delta-V and the fit cost are computed. Features: the mean and the median of the four delta-V values, the delta-V of the minimum-cost family, a cost-weighted mean (weights exp(-cost / (median cost + 1e-6))), and the disagreement (standard deviation of log delta-V over families). Baselines "our implementation of the idea": (i) the triangle family alone (the triangular-reconstruction idea), (ii) a Gaussian-process regression over the unclipped samples of the pulse (RBF kernel plus noise, hyper-parameters by marginal likelihood, clipped samples replaced by the posterior mean with the posterior mean floored at the clip level), integrated by the same severity code.

**A4 tail-aware estimator.** Features: A1 descriptors, A3 features, the plain delta-V, pulse width and clipped fraction (the three features of the earlier ridge). Models fitted on the training tests of each fold: (a) the earlier ridge on its three features (reproduces M+), (b) a ridge on the full feature set predicting log(reference / fill estimate), (c) gradient boosting (scikit-learn `GradientBoostingRegressor`, 100 trees, depth 2, learning rate 0.05, subsample 0.8, seed 42) predicting the same target, (d) quantile gradient boosting for the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of the same target (same settings, `loss="quantile"`). The point estimate of the "tail-aware estimator" is the median-quantile model (d, 50th). Hyper-parameters are fixed here; no tuning. Events that never clip are returned as the plain estimate (as M+ does). Compared with plain, spline, fill, fill + ridge (M+), the triangular-fit baseline and the Gaussian-process baseline, on all four splits and three ranges. Metrics: median, 90th and 95th percentile absolute error, share within +-10%, paired differences with grouped bootstrap.

**A5 adaptive conformal intervals.** Target level 90% (alpha 0.10); the 5-fold structure of R with a vehicle-grouped calibration share of 0.4 of the training groups (as in the UA-SCT study). Intervals are on the multiplicative error log(reference / estimate): (i) plain split conformal on the absolute residual of the median model; (ii) conformalized quantile regression from the 10th and 90th percentile models (lower and upper end of the interval, widened by the usual CQR correction); (iii) a normalised-score variant: the absolute log residual divided by a predicted difficulty, where difficulty is a gradient-boosting model (same settings) of the absolute log residual fitted on the non-calibration training tests, floored at 0.02. Report coverage on held-out vehicles per range, and mean width (km/h and relative) and coverage per **clipped-fraction tercile** (terciles of the clipped fraction among clipped events, cut points from training tests; events that never clip form their own bin, "none").

**A6 abstention flag.** Flag an event when its clipped fraction exceeds the 75th percentile of the training clipped fractions among clipped events, or its predicted interval width (variant iii) exceeds the 75th percentile of training widths. Report the number and share flagged, the median and 90th-percentile error of unflagged and flagged events, and the share of Severe or Critical reference events among the flagged.

**Hypotheses for A (primary range +-16 g, primary split R, primary estimator = A4 (d) median model, comparison = M+).**
- **H-A1.** The tail-aware estimator lowers the 90th-percentile delta-V error relative to M+ at +-16 g on split R by at least 5 points (point estimate of p90(M+) minus p90(tail-aware) at least 5), with the 95% grouped interval of the difference excluding zero, and the point estimate of the p90 difference is a reduction of at least 5 points on at least two of the four splits (R, G, A, O). p-value: share of grouped resamples with a reduction at or below 0. Counts against: the interval includes zero, or the gain of at least 5 points appears on only one split.
- **H-A2.** For clipped events in the lowest clipped-fraction tercile, the normalised-score adaptive interval (A5 iii) is narrower than the plain split-conformal interval (A5 i) (mean relative width difference below zero; p-value: share of grouped resamples with a difference at or above 0), and its coverage on held-out vehicles at +-16 g is within 2 points of 0.90 (that is, between 0.88 and 0.92) for the whole test set. Counts against: no narrowing, or coverage outside the band.
- **H-A3.** The bounds of A2 contain the reference delta-V for at least 90% of the 176 tests at +-16 g. p-value: share of grouped resamples with containment below 0.90. Counts against: containment below 0.90 (point estimate), reported with the cases where the assumptions fail.
- Secondary (EXPLORATORY): everything at +-32 and +-64 g, all other comparisons in A4, the quantile coverage, A6.

## 2. Study D: clip-onset early severity flag (MEASURED_SOFTWARE, 176 NHTSA tests)

**Data.** Same tests, sensor model and splits as Study A (primary split R, repeated grouped 5-fold, 10 repeats; also G, A, O), at +-16, +-32, +-64 g and 100 and 200 Hz. Primary: +-16 g, 100 Hz. Reference severity class: maximum of the delta-V class and the ASI class from the reference delta-V and the reference ASI of the unclipped record (limits 24/40/56 km/h and 1.0/1.4/1.9 g, as in `ua_core`). Severe or Critical = class 2 or 3. Time zero = the trigger sample. "Full-pulse decision time" = the pulse length of the severity code (`pulse_ms` of the plain estimate; the time at which the full-pulse class can be known).

**D1 onset features.** Time from the trigger to the earliest sample with |a| at or above q x range on either horizontal axis; the rise slope over the 3 samples before that sample; whether any sample reaches that level in the pulse (clipping occurs at all). q in {0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.98}.

**D2 flag rule.** Flag Severe or Critical when the onset time is at most T_on ms (flag time = onset time). (q, T_on) are chosen inside each training fold by grouped cross-validation on the training tests only (inner grouped 4-fold on make/model): over q in the set above and T_on in 10-ms steps from 10 ms to 300 ms, choose the pair that maximises the detection rate of reference Severe or Critical tests subject to an over-triage (share of reference Low or Moderate tests flagged) of at most 0.05 on the inner validation folds; ties go to the smaller T_on, then the larger q.

**D3 evaluation and comparators** (each is a stand-alone Severe/Critical flagger with a flag time or no flag):
 (i) the full-pulse class of the earlier estimator (fill + ridge delta-V and the ASI of the filled window, class 2 or 3), flag time = full-pulse decision time;
 (ii) **the severity analogue of the earlier early-exit rule.** The N19 early-exit rule classifies crash against non-crash on VZCrash windows with forests; it cannot be applied to NHTSA severity without new training, so the analogue is a sequential rule on the running plain delta-V of the clipped window: flag Severe or Critical at the earliest sample where the running delta-V (same integration as the severity code) reaches the Severe limit of 40 km/h. This is the early-exit idea applied to severity; it is labelled "analogue", not "the earlier rule";
 (iii) an early-peak threshold without the clipping logic: flag when the resultant peak acceleration within the opening T_pk ms after the trigger reaches G_pk g; (T_pk, G_pk) chosen by the same inner cross-validation and criterion as D2, T_pk in {50, 100, 150, 200, 300} ms, G_pk in {2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12} g (reported only where G_pk is below the range).
 For each: detection rate of reference Severe or Critical tests (1 minus under-triage), over-triage, flag time, time saved (full-pulse decision time minus flag time, flagged true positives only), and the share of events that never clip (and therefore get no early flag from D2). Events not flagged early fall back to (i); the under-triage and over-triage reported for D2 are those of the early flag alone.

**Hypotheses for D (primary: +-16 g, 100 Hz, split R, q and T_on chosen as above).**
- **H-D1.** Among reference Severe or Critical tests flagged by the D2 rule, the median time saved against the full-pulse decision is at least 100 ms (lower end of the grouped interval at or above 100 ms; p-value: share of grouped resamples with a median below 100 ms), AND the under-triage rate of the D2 flag is not worse than that of the analogue (ii): the 95% grouped interval of (under-triage of D2 minus under-triage of (ii)) does not lie entirely above zero. Counts against: the time saved is below 100 ms, or that interval lies entirely above zero.
- **H-D2.** The over-triage of the D2 flag (reference Low or Moderate tests flagged) is below 10% on split R. p-value: share of grouped resamples with over-triage at or above 0.10.
- Reported plainly: events that never clip at +-16 g cannot be flagged this way; their share is reported for every range and rate.

## 3. Study B: load-adaptive escalation (SIMULATED)

**Question.** The equal-escalation test (T1) showed that the interval class rule does no better than lowering the table's threshold. This study tests whether *where* the escalations go matters, not how many.

**Simulator.** `sct_sim` with the same payloads (97 B alert, 14 B flash and CANCEL, 26 B ACK), signed ACK, retries, spacing, preemption and duty cap (1% trailing hour, not verified for India), with these additions, written so that the existing configurations give bit-identical results (a regression test against stored `s11` cells is part of the deliverable):
 - the class of an alert is decided at the decision time (arrival + decision delay + compute time) from the pool entry's delta-V and ASI estimates and a threshold scale s, as class(dv / s, ASI / s) with the limits of `ua_core` (s = 1 is the table's default thresholds; s below 1 lowers them);
 - a per-alert flag `extra` = the point class at s = 1 is below Severe while the class at the car's current s is Severe or Critical (an escalation of an event the default table would not escalate);
 - a metric `escalation_used`: the share of true Low or Moderate crash alerts that used the fast path at least once.
 Class pairs come from the NHTSA cross-validated delta-V and ASI estimates at +-16 g (the folds of `ua_core`), with the true class from the reference. Only +-16 g is simulated.

**Starting point.** Every adaptive variant starts at s0 = f, the factor found in T1 for +-16 g (0.6375), which gives the escalation of P3 (0.683 of non-severe events); it can only move s up toward 1 (fewer escalations). The matched fixed baseline of each variant is the same rule with a constant s_b in [s0, 1].

**B1 matched baselines.** For each cell and variant: run the variant, take its mean realised `escalation_used` over the 40 seeds, find s_b by bisection on the pool-level escalation (pool share of true Low or Moderate entries with class at s_b at or above Severe; tolerance 0.005, 40 iterations), run the fixed rule at s_b over the same seeds, and report the match error (realised escalation of baseline minus variant, mean and interval). Also run in every cell: P1 and P2 at default thresholds (s = 1), the fixed lowered-threshold rule at s0 (equal to the P3 escalation) and the severity table with spaced retries (P1b of S13).

**B2 adaptive variants** (parameters fixed here unless listed as tuned; tuned values are chosen on the tuning scenarios only, from the stated grid, by the largest mean critical reach within 10 s over the burst tuning scenarios at the escalation-matched baseline, ties to the simpler setting):
 1. **Controller.** Per car, an exponentially weighted failure rate F of the car's own alert rounds (failure = the listen window ended without an ACK; weight 0.3 per round; F decays toward 0 with a 3,600 s time constant). The scale s moves by a bounded step g (tuned over {0.02, 0.05, 0.10}) up when F exceeds 0.5 and down when F falls below 0.2 (hysteresis: no change in between), within [s0, 1.0], evaluated at each decision.
 2. **Token bucket.** Per car, a bucket of capacity b = 1 token (b in {1, 2} tuned) refilled at r tokens per hour (r in {0.5, 1, 2, 4} tuned). An *extra* escalation consumes a token and is admitted only if a token is available; otherwise the event keeps its default-table class (s = 1). Critical assignments (class 3) never need a token. Classes are assigned at s0.
 3. **Beacon-assisted.** The base broadcasts a 9-byte beacon (1 level byte plus an 8-byte tag) whenever its load level changes and at least every 120 s. Load level = 0 if fewer than 1, 1 if 1 to 4, 2 if 5 to 14, 3 if 15 or more alert or flash messages were received in the last 60 s. A car that has heard a beacon within 300 s uses s = s0 + (1 - s0) x level / 3, otherwise s0. Beacons are transmitted by the base under its own duty window (they count against the base's duty budget and block its receiver while on air); each car hears a beacon with probability 1 minus the downlink loss.
 4. **Severity-scaled backoff.** No change to s (= s0, so the class equals the fixed rule at s0); every transmission of an alert is delayed by a uniform random time from 0 to W_c with W_c = 2.0, 1.0, 0.5, 0.25 s for classes 0 to 3.
 5. **Graceful demotion.** s = s0. An alert with the `extra` flag whose class is Severe (not Critical) is demoted to the normal path (the existing `demoted` mechanism) after k failed rounds, k in {1, 2, 3} tuned. Class 3 is never demoted.

**B3 scenarios** (40 seeds per cell; one factor at a time from the default of 100 cars, SF7, 30-car burst, capture 6 dB, loss 0.1, duty 1%):
 - *Burst cells, unloaded (7):* base, burst of 10, burst of 60, 50 cars, 200 cars, SF9, capture none.
 - *Burst cells, loaded (3):* the base, 200-car and SF9 cells with background crash alerts at 1.0 per car per hour whose pool is resampled so that 80% of the events are true Low or Moderate (heavy non-severe escalation competing with the burst of critical alerts).
 - *Steady cells (6):* base, 50 cars, 200 cars, SF9, capture none, loss 0.3.
 - *Single-car cell (1):* one car, 20 alerts per hour, loss 0.6 both ways, no other traffic.
 - *Mixed cell (1):* steady at 200 cars with the loaded background (no burst).
 Known limitation, written before the runs: in the unloaded burst cells almost no non-severe event is escalated during the burst (the background alert rate is 0.1 per car per hour and each car has at most one alert), and the per-car controller and token bucket have no history to act on, so no gain is expected there; the loaded cells and the mixed cell are where competing escalations exist. The unloaded cells are kept as part of the majority rule below because they are the cells of the earlier sweep.

**B4 metrics.** Primary: critical reach within 10 s (share of true Critical alerts acknowledged within 10 s) in the burst cells, at matched escalation. Secondary: critical reach within 30 s, critical 95th-percentile latency, mis-triage, delivery of true non-severe alerts (they must not be starved: reported as `delivered_normal`), airtime and duty use, runs above the duty cap (must be zero), the extra base airtime of the beacon variant.

**B5 sensitivity.** One-at-a-time sweeps of the controller step and thresholds, bucket rate, demotion k and beacon period (60, 120, 300 s) on the burst cells; the number of cells in which each variant beats its matched baseline (whole interval above zero). EXPLORATORY.

**Hypotheses for B** (difference = variant minus its matched fixed baseline, paired by seed; "gain" in points of critical reach within 10 s).
- **H-B1.** In the burst cells (10), at least one variant has a gain of at least 3 points with the 95% interval above zero in a majority of them (at least 6 of 10). p-value for the Holm family: for the variant with the most such cells, the sixth smallest of its ten one-sided seed-bootstrap p-values (the largest of the six needed), multiplied by 5 (Bonferroni over variants), capped at 1. Counts against: no variant does, or the gain appears only in the cell with the weakest baseline (the cell where the matched baseline has the lowest critical reach).
- **H-B2.** No variant lowers critical reach within 10 s by more than 2 points, and none raises mis-triage by more than 2 points, in the steady cells and the single-car cell, relative to the matched baseline (non-inferiority: the upper end of the one-sided 95% bound of the harm is below 2 points). p-value: the largest of the one-sided non-inferiority p-values over variants, cells and the two metrics (an intersection test; no further correction inside it). Counts against: any variant with a harm above 2 points whose interval excludes 2.
- **H-B3.** Decided only if H-B1 holds. The mean gain over the burst cells of the token bucket is at least 80% of the mean gain of the best variant. No p-value; point rule. If H-B1 does not hold, H-B3 is reported "not evaluable" and counted as not supported.
- The Holm family contains H-B1 and H-B2 only; the 50 variant-by-cell tests are shown in full.

## 4. Study E: authentication-tag length against capacity (ANALYTICAL plus SIMULATED)

**E1 forgery model (ANALYTICAL).** Tag lengths t in {4, 6, 8, 12, 16} bytes, a truncated HMAC-SHA256 with a secret key (the attacker's best attack is guessing; no key recovery or truncation weakness). Probability of one forgery within n independent attempts: 1 - (1 - 2^(-8t))^n. Attempts needed for success probability 1e-6: ln(1 - 1e-6) / ln(1 - 2^(-8t)). Attempt rate: a single transmitter limited by the duty cycle: floor(0.01 x 3,600 s / airtime) alert frames per hour, airtime from the Semtech formula (125 kHz, coding rate 4/5, explicit header, CRC on) for the alert of (89 + t) bytes at SF7, SF8, SF9 (the alert body is 89 bytes). Expected time to one forgery at that rate; the uncapped rate (back-to-back frames) is shown as a sensitivity; the duty limit does not bind an attacker, which is stated. Work factor = expected attempts 2^(8t - 1) (as log2). No claim of cryptographic proof; truncating a tag is a known trade-off and the contribution is the curve for this protocol.

**E2 capacity effect (SIMULATED).** The simulator gets message lengths: alert 89 + t, flash 14 - (8 - t) = 6 + t (flash = 6-byte header plus tag), CANCEL the same as flash, ACK unchanged (EXPLORATORY variant: ACK 18 + t). Policy P2 (SCT) and P1 at the tuned parameters of S11, all seven unloaded burst cells and the three loaded burst cells of Study B, 40 seeds, point classes at s = 1. Metrics: critical reach within 10 s and 30 s, share of the duty budget used, collided share.

**E3.** One table and one figure (tag bytes against log2 forgery work factor and critical reach within 10 s). The smallest tag whose duty-capped forgery work factor at SF9 stays above 2^32 attempts is stated, with the assumptions.

**Hypotheses for E.**
- **H-E1.** Going from 8 to 4 bytes raises critical reach within 10 s in the burst cells (the 7 unloaded cells, mean of paired seed differences, P2) by at least 2 points with a 95% interval above zero. p-value: one-sided seed bootstrap of the mean over cells (seeds resampled inside each cell). Counts against: no gain; this would justify keeping 8 bytes or more.
- **H-E2.** An 8-byte tag keeps the forgery work factor above 2^32 attempts under the duty-capped attacker at SF7 to SF9: that is, attempts for success probability 1e-6 and the expected attempts are both above 2^32. Formula; no p-value. Counts against: either is not.

## 5. Study F: severity-dependent hold (ANALYTICAL plus SIMULATED)

**F1 cost model (ANALYTICAL).** For an alert of class c with hold h (0 to 5 s, grid of 0.5 s): expected cost = p x D_c x h + (1 - p) x C_false x R(h), where p = share of alerts that are real, D_c = C_delay x w_c is the delay cost per second for a real event of class c (w = 0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0 for classes 0 to 3; C_delay is that of Critical), C_false the cost of a false alert reaching the base, and R(h) = 0.1 + 0.9 x clip((4 - h) / 3, 0, 1) the chance a false alert is still sent after hold h (the driver cancels a false alert with probability 0.9, uniformly 1 to 4 s after the decision, the cancel model of `sct_sim`; a cancel inside the hold prevents transmission). p is the precision of the deployed forest at 0.02% prevalence (`s10.rf15.pi0p02pct.a.ppv`, 0.0043) as the default, and the mix of the simulator's false-alarm cell (0.1 real per 0.25 false alerts per car-hour) as a sensitivity. Grid of the ratio r = C_delay / C_false (per second): 1, 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000. Report the optimal hold per class and ratio, and the cost curve. The ratio grid, the class weights and the cancel model are assumptions fixed here.

**F2 simulation check (SIMULATED).** The simulator gets a hold parameter for Critical (`h_c`, default 0.0, so existing runs are unchanged) and the hold of Severe `h_s` (tuned value in S11: 0.0 s). Compared in the steady cell with false alarms at the 5% target (0.25 per car-hour) and the same with the 1% target (0.05 per car-hour), and in the loaded burst cells with false alarms: (a) the current rule (Critical 0 s, Severe 0 s as tuned), (b) a short positive Severe hold of 2.0 s (Critical 0 s) (the SctParams default; 1 s and 3 s are secondary), (c) the model-optimal holds at r = 100 from F1, (d) a flat hold of 2.0 s for all classes. 40 seeds. Metrics: critical reach within 10 s and p95 latency, share of false alarms of assigned class Severe whose alert or flash reached the base, the same for all false alarms, and the expected-cost value of F1 with the simulated shares.

**Hypotheses for F.**
- **H-F1.** For Critical alerts the model-optimal hold is zero for every ratio in the grid (1 to 10,000) under both values of p. Formula; no p-value. Counts against: any ratio with a positive optimal hold. (The structure of the cost model makes the optimum 0 or about 4 s, so this is a statement about where the crossing ratio lies.)
