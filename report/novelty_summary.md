### VZCrash paper and dataset card (read in full)
- Paper: Bianconcini et al., "VZCrash: A Large-Scale IMU Dataset of Ego-Vehicle Crashes", arXiv 2606.06074 (June 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06074.
- Splits: the paper states the splits are "strictly separated by vehicle". It does not mention duplicate recordings, and the release has no vehicle ID, so this cannot be checked.
- Triggers: recordings "were triggered on-device based on multiple threshold heuristics" (hard braking, hard acceleration, shock events) and then processed in the cloud. Our trigger is therefore a second trigger on events that were already selected once.
- Near-miss: counted as a negative class. No false-positive rate for near-miss events alone is reported. The paper does report false-positive counts on a 735,000-event real-world population of harsh-driving events (Section III-D).
- Microcontroller: not covered. Latency is measured on a server CPU core, and the paper says the models "could be deployed in most modern embedded devices".
- Direction and severity labels are listed as future work.
- Table I of the paper gives results on full 16 s events with server-side models. Our windows are 2.8 s after a trigger, so we do not compare with it.
- Dataset card: https://huggingface.co/datasets/vzc-research-chapter/VZCrash (no mention of duplicates, trigger stage, or microcontrollers).

## N14 saturation-aware severity estimator: prior-work search (2026-11-11)

Queries (standard web search, 2026-11-11): "clipped accelerometer signal reconstruction crash pulse saturation delta-V estimation half-sine fit"; "crash severity delta-V estimation from low-cost MEMS accelerometer smartphone IMU range sampling rate saturation ASI".

Opened and read:
- **US 7,643,921 B2, "Clipped sensor data estimator"**, Andres and McConnell, Continental Automotive Systems US, filed 2005-08-26, granted 2010-01-05 (the PDF has no text layer; read through OCR). Abstract in the sources checked: "estimating values beyond sensor limits" by determining "an actual slope" from measured data and then "an estimated slope ... as a percentage of the actual slope" for the samples outside the limits until the measured values return inside. It cites a clipping-restoration page from audio processing (Gdansk University of Technology). So estimating the values of a clipped vehicle sensor signal is not new. It extrapolates with a slope; our method fits a pulse model (half-sine, haversine or triangle) to the unsaturated rise and fall and then integrates for delta-V.
- **Tadic and Vukajlovic, Radioengineering 25(1), 2016** (already in the N13 record): low-G MEMS sensors "were mostly saturated" in a real crash and were combined with high-G sensors; saturation is handled by using a wider-range sensor, not by reconstruction.
- **Westrom, Adanty and Shimada, SAE 2025-01-5046**, "Pulse and polynomial functions to predict vehicle acceleration using event data recorder delta-V in frontal and rear-end collisions" (abstract page read): triangular, half-sine and haversine pulse functions and polynomials are regressed to EDR delta-V to obtain acceleration. This is the same family of pulse functions, used to go from delta-V to acceleration, not to repair clipped data. No mention of clipping in the abstract.

Seen as a search snippet only (not opened, not used): "Evaluation of a restoration algorithm applied to clipped tibial acceleration signals" (Sensors 2023): restoration of clipped acceleration in a sports-biomechanics setting.

Closest work: US 7,643,921 (clipped data estimate by slope extrapolation in a vehicle sensor) and the pulse-function regressions of SAE 2025-01-5046. Not found in the sources checked: a pulse-model fill of a saturated crash pulse that is then scored on delta-V against crash-test reference values with a held-out split. The novelty claim, if the held-out result clears its interval, is limited to that evaluation on a 100 Hz, 16 g sensor chain; the idea of estimating clipped data is established. ## 4. Draft novelty paragraph

We build and evaluate an integrated crash-alert system: an ESP32 detector, physics-based severity and authenticated LoRa delivery. The software side is done; hardware tests are pending. System recall including the trigger is 0.900 [0.892 to 0.909] (mean of 5 seeds) after removing 150 test events that also occur in train or validation (217 cross-split groups were found), and 0.873 on a test set that also drops window-level near-duplicates (EXPLORATORY); precision at a realistic crash prevalence would be low. At +-16 g a pulse-fit fill with a ridge correction lowers the median delta-V error from 25.7% (plain) and 23.5% (spline restoration) to 11.9%, with no practically meaningful gain at `+-32 g` or `+-64 g` and no blind test. Impact direction, radio delivery, scale-model transfer and ESP32 timing await hardware results.

## 4b. Layered view

**Framing: what a low-cost crash node must measure.** The points fall into four layers.

- **Sensing and restoration (N13, N14).** A wide-range sensor, or a pulse-fit fill with a ridge correction when the sensor clips. Fill + ridge was named primary after earlier results were seen, and NHTSA tests are mostly high-speed barrier tests and are not representative of all real crashes.
- **Edge decision and prevalence (N9, N25, N19).** System recall includes the trigger. Precision at a realistic prevalence is 0.0043 with the existing threshold, and no tested rule fixes it within five points of recall. The early-exit rule decides about one second earlier; its recall gain is an ensemble effect.
- **Link and delivery (N26, N16, N22, N28).** In simulated bursts a fast path with a short flash changes critical reach within ten seconds by 0.247 against the severity table and by 0.172 against a severity table with spaced retries, with no gain in steady state. Taking the class from the upper end of a conformal interval changes mis-triage by -0.048 and sends many more non-severe events to the fast path; with the thresholds of the plain table lowered to the same escalation, the difference is -0.003 (no benefit beyond the thresholds).
- **Authentication and protocol checks (N3, N12, N27).** Signed alerts and ACKs, a counter and a re-ACK, checked by an attack suite and by a bounded model check of a model (not a proof of the firmware).

Every result that does not clear its interval is reported as a negative result.

## 5. Hardware plan and what it needs to move each point to SUPPORTED

The plan is the author's minimum set. **H1** impact side: 10 hits on each of 4 sides and 10 vertical drops (N2). **H2** crash against not-crash: the H1 hits plus 30 non-crashes (taps, hard stops, ramp bumps, lift-and-place), and the transfer test from VZCrash (N1, SF). **H3** LoRa: 20 alerts each at 10, 50 and 100 m, with an optional wall (N4, N5). **H4** security: forged, tampered, replay, spoofed ACK, base reboot and ACK-lost retry, 3 tries each (N3, N12). **H5** edge cost: compile report and logged microseconds (N1, N3). **H6** SW-420 ablation: re-analysis of H1 and H2 (N7).

**Limitations by design.** Severity measured against impact strength and the 200 m LoRa runs were dropped from the plan, so the work cannot claim a measured severity response or a range beyond 100 m.
