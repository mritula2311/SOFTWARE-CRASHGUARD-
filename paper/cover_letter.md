To the Editor, IEEE Access

Title: Measuring, Deciding and Reporting Crashes on Constrained LPWAN Nodes: Benchmark Audit, Sensing Bounds, Formally Verified Alerting and Trace-Driven Evaluation

We submit this manuscript for consideration as a regular paper in IEEE Access.

**What the paper adds.** It evaluates the components of a crash-alert node on a constrained LPWAN link (a stated profile of memory, airtime and energy assumptions) in one preregistered study, with an evidence label on every result, and it reports every one of the 138 preregistered hypotheses in 15 versions (48 supported, 72 not supported, 4 partly supported, 13 not run). It reports (C1) a duplicate audit of a public crash dataset (217 cross-split groups of identical arrays; recall at the 1% false-alarm target goes from 0.631 to 0.558 when duplicates are removed); (C2) the accelerometer range that delta-V needs by crash mode (+-32 g for frontal-barrier and +-64 g for pole pulses); (C3) a compact detector trade-off; (C4) Tamarin proofs of alert authenticity, per-vehicle acknowledgement binding and freshness properties for a per-vehicle-key design, the acknowledgement-suppression attack on the shared-key design, and a ProVerif result: alert-identifier reuse when a car reboots inside the same second, with a boot-counter fix proved in the symbolic model; (C5) a two-stage alert whose provisional decision comes a median 1750 ms earlier on real pulses, with its airtime cost; (C6) an evaluation of retry policies in two simulators and on measured LoRaWAN traces (an evaluation finding, not a new policy); (C7) deployment requirements from simulated hardware-format logs, a mounting calibration with its limit, and a wake-on-motion estimate. Where the paper says that nothing was found, it means: no work found in the sources checked (IEEE Xplore, OpenAlex, Crossref, DOAJ, publisher pages; Google Scholar blocked automated queries and was not searched). LoRa and LoRaWAN protocols have been formally analysed before (key establishment and user authentication); the formal claims here cover crash-alert frames only. Carneiro et al. (NOMS 2023) already report delivery ratio and latency for a LoRa crash-response system in ns-3; the gap is a stated security analysis, an authenticated acknowledgement and formal verification.

**Round 6.** An 8-byte micro-confirm that replaces the 97-byte confirm after an acknowledged provisional frame uses 0.46 to 0.77 of the two-stage alert's airtime in the 16 ns-3 cells (at most 0.77 per delivered alert at SF9), with delivery within 0.0025 of it in those cells; the trace groups give a zero difference by construction and are not evidence for delivery. The simulated frame has 13 bytes against the 8-byte design, which stays within the evaluated size (at SF7 it costs the airtime of the 14-byte flash, so the saving is against the 97-byte confirm only), and its extra airtime against the single-stage alert, which is negative, is not offered as a gain: the 40% criterion was nearly automatic. Its authenticity, binding and cancel lemmas are proved in ProVerif and Tamarin in symbolic models, and a hash ratchet is proved in ProVerif within 3 ratchet steps. A quarantine rule that counts frames per claimed identifier before verification is shown attackable by an attacker without a key with 2 frames in a bounded model; the fix is two counters, and the rule that counts after verification holds by construction. A sequential early-exit rule decides 14.2 ms earlier and raises withdrawn provisional alerts by 12.6 points; it is not supported, and the preregistration quoted the fixed rule's mean decision time wrongly (720 to 780 ms against 112 ms), which is disclosed. The decision path was built for the target as build artifacts: 15,771 bytes of forest data, 33,559 bytes of linked flash (including unused functions) and a 1,568-byte stack chain; instruction counts, timing and energy are NOT_RUN. The supplement gives the byte layout of every frame and a table of 16 abstractions between the layout, the formal models and ns-3. The Indian 433 MHz rules (G.S.R. 347(E), 2022) allow 10 mW e.r.p. at a duty cycle of at most 10%, with the observation interval not stated; the evaluation used a stricter 1% hourly allowance, and a deployed node would need type approval. A duty-lockout condition shows that the stored two-stage results, produced under an hourly airtime allowance, do not transfer to a region that enforces a per-transmission off-time on one sub-band.

**Round 7.** Two additions to the protocol work; of five preregistered hypotheses, two are supported and three are not. (1) An attacker that suppresses acknowledgements makes the node spend airtime on retries. A reserved airtime budget for the confirm or cancel frame was checked in a bounded model (1,139,264 configurations per policy: no late follow-up while the reserve is intact, 162,752 late runs for the shipped policy) and in simulation; at the load that exhausts the shipped policy under a 1% allowance the one-event reserve keeps the follow-up on time in 70.4%, 26.8% and 35.2% of runs at SF7, SF8 and SF9, so the 99% target is not met; a reserve for four events meets it (post-run). The reserve costs 2.4 points of delivery in the high-loss trace group under a per-transmission off-time (bound: 1 point). (2) A keyed rotating pseudonym replaces the vehicle id; ProVerif proves that two alerts of one vehicle cannot be told from two vehicles (also for unboundedly many alerts), the fixed-id design fails, and the earlier lemmas still hold with the pseudonym. Resolution costs at most 23 microseconds per frame; the collision bound of 1e-6 is not met at the frame's 2 or 4 bytes (0.216 and 3.7e-06). Position, timing and radio fingerprints stay visible; nothing is measured on hardware. Related work: the pseudonym idea is from Pélissier et al. (WiSec 2024), who argue unlinkability informally; the energy-depletion attacks on LoRaWAN are prior work. Contributions stay eight.

**Hypotheses.** 138 hypotheses were preregistered in 15 versions; 125 were run, 48 are supported, 72 not supported, 4 partly supported and 13 not run (one hypothesis of round 7 is descriptive and has no verdict). The ledger lists every row.
<!-- ledger:end -->

**What is negative.** The paper reports its negative results in full (the main table in the paper and a list of all 68 in the release archive, and every hypothesis with its verdict in the supplement): the advantage of an adaptive retry table over a severity table is +0.116 in the custom simulator and +0.021 in ns-3, and on measured traces a control that retries at once delivers 54.0% against 49.3% for the table; the two-stage alert costs 37% extra airtime at SF7 and 54% at SF9 against a 40% design bound; no synthetic-pulse generator passed the realism gate; trigger-aware training, conformal class rules, short frames, overhear-and-suppress retries, slotted fast paths, receiver diversity and a mirror-flip test did not meet their preregistered criteria. The car-reboot identifier lemma was not decided in Tamarin and is reported as such.

**Limits.** No measurement on physical hardware is reported, and none is claimed: the sizes of the decision path are build artifacts for the ESP32 target, not board measurements; hardware results are collected separately by the author. The results are statements about code run on public data, simulations, replayed traces, proofs and formulas, each with its label.

**Venue.** We suggest IEEE Access; IEEE Open Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems is an alternative if the editors prefer a transport-systems readership.

**Why IEEE Access.** The paper is a long, evaluation-heavy systems study that spans sensing, embedded detection, wireless protocol design and formal checking, and it gives space to negative results and to the assumptions behind them. These are the kinds of material a broad-scope open-access journal can carry in full.

**Data and code.** All simulation and analysis code, the formal models, the ns-3 patch, the preregistrations, the hypothesis ledger and the derived data will be released (code under Apache-2.0; derived VZCrash data under CC BY-NC 4.0 with attribution; raw third-party data are not redistributed). A Zenodo DOI will be added on release.

**Round 7b.** The attack of round 7 (acknowledgement suppression against a duty budget) is a known class; the abstract of Chen, Ben-Othman and Mokdad (IEEE Communications Letters 2023) describes denial-of-service attacks on confirmed transmission that use the duty-cycle limit, and the paper claims the defence, the bounded model check and the preregistered results, not the attack. The reserve for one event failed its 99% target at a 1% allowance; a reserve for four events, preregistered afterwards (V19) and tested on fresh seeds, meets it in all nine cells, and its benign delivery cost in the high-loss trace group under a per-transmission off-time (2.4 points) exceeds the 1-point bound. The quarantine lemma with pseudonyms holds in its injective form only if the base applies the seen-set atomically (Tamarin model; ProVerif reports a race). Transmit power: the conservative reading is used, so the link results are an optimistic bound on margin by up to 4 dB.
